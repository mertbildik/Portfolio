# Case-study craft — how the best current portfolios show work

From a study of 12+ current individual portfolios (design engineers and product designers,
verified live Aug 2026) plus 2025–26 hiring-manager commentary. This file answers: how much
process each project needs, what to show instead of explain, and how the three case studies
should differ.

## The models that matter (and what each proves)

- **Emil Kowalski (emilkowal.ski, Linear).** No classic case studies — essays about small
  shipped things, with **live demos embedded mid-read** ("Add toast" buttons you press while
  reading). Every micro-decision gets a because-clause, including admitted trial-and-error:
  "0.11 is just a number that I ended up on through trial and error." Credibility entirely
  from side projects; zero client logos.
- **Rauno Freiberg (rauno.me/craft, Vercel).** A chronological grid: looping clip thumbnail,
  title, date, one link. **Scannable in 30 seconds, deep on demand** — the best
  "60-second scan, 5-minute reward" architecture found. Captions describe mechanics, never
  adjectives.
- **Gloria Lo (glorialo.design, Principal at Canva — hired off this portfolio).** The best
  classic case study. What elevates it above template: named constraints ("I am half the
  design team" — 2 designers vs 70+ engineers), a visible prioritization formula, and honest
  qualitative results instead of invented numbers. Her Sealadder study is the masterclass in
  no-results honesty: product never launched, so the results section is a founder testimonial,
  competition placements, and skills — and the transparency strengthens it.
- **Steph Parrott (stephparrott.com/plantd).** The best small-project case study found: solo
  app, no client, no metrics — reads senior through stakes ("As someone who hasn't eaten meat
  in almost 20 years…"), one sharp research insight, before/afters each carrying one reason,
  and full shipping detail (React Native, Firebase, 9 months). Ex-Square credentials sit in
  the footer, not the headline.
- **Brian Lovin (brianlovin.com, App Dissection).** Annotated-screenshot narration: captions
  do all the talking. The format that proves judgment with minimal prose.
- **Benji Taylor (benji.org, ex-Coinbase, now SpaceX).** Senior credibility from a stream of
  small, finished, *current* things with short conversational write-ups — dated Jan–Jul 2026.
  Recency as a trust signal.

Dropped from study: agency-style sites, gated portfolios, "coming soon" case studies.

## The structural finding: decision spine, not process skeleton

The Research→Define→Ideate→Prototype template is now actively harmful — hiring managers skip
it ("they see 'Empathize' and their brain says 'I know this part, scroll down'" — Emily
Backes, Jul 2026), and AI tools literally generate it, so it reads as machine output.

The replacement, consistent across the best examples and the hiring commentary:

- **Each section is a decision, not a phase**: context → constraint → decision → tradeoff →
  what changed.
- **Story shape**: the situation → the problem → where the plan broke → what shipped and what
  changed. "The wireframes are evidence. The story is the case study."
- **First 5 seconds state role + what changed.** "A reviewer can't credit you for a decision
  they can't find" — 'we' language and vague ownership are the #1 credibility killer.
- Visible friction is a feature: "A portfolio with zero visible friction anywhere doesn't
  read as accomplished. It reads as edited." (This validates the plan's rule: reversals appear
  where they actually happened, never as a template.)

## Presentation: what to show instead of explain

- **Live embeds beat screenshots, screenshots with captions beat paragraphs.** As someone who
  ships the sites, Mert has the Emil/Rauno option almost nobody else has: embed the live
  thing, or a working fragment of it. A coded demo in the page outranks a logo in a PNG.
- **Text:visual calibration**: classic case studies that work run ~2,000–2,500 words at
  roughly 60:40 text:visual. Design-engineer portfolios invert to 20:80. The read window is
  55 seconds to 3 minutes, first 5 seconds decisive.
- **Captions narrate; sections stay 150–400 words.** Rauno's captions describe what happens
  mechanically. One reason per before/after pair, placed at the decision point in the story —
  never a gallery dump at the end.
- **Two-layer architecture**: a 60-second card (title, role, one-line claim, the delta) opening
  into the full story. This matches the homepage-as-index decision already made.
- **Apply UX to the case study itself**: summary up top, hierarchy, progressive disclosure.
  Dense paragraphs are a design failure on a designer's site.

## Small clients read as senior when…

1. The constraint is stated like scope, not apology: "solo designer-developer, one month,
   AI-assisted build" is a scoping statement.
2. There's a specific delta, even without metrics: a thing that exists now and didn't before,
   a client quote, a before/after of the actual business artifact. Absence of any delta is the
   red flag; modest scale is not.
3. The stakes are real and stated plainly (Parrott's 20-years-vegetarian line).
4. Honesty about what didn't happen (Sealadder's unlaunched product) replaces manufactured
   results.

## How the three case studies should differ (synthesis)

| | Sinerjik | OFK | Adclusive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Emil/Rauno: demo-led, 20:80 toward visuals, live embeds | Gloria Lo/Rokt: constraint-led story, ~60:40 | Sealadder/App Dissection: honest artifacts, captions narrate, shortest |
| Spine | Taste + measurement decisions, each with a because-clause | Chronology of decisions with a real reversal | Years + category, screens as history |
| Reader touches | The live site, /tasarim, motion | The EN/PL toggle clip, reference PDFs | Wireframe→shipped side-by-side |

The three shouldn't share a template — each project's evidence dictates its form. That's
itself a design argument the portfolio makes silently.

## One counter-current worth knowing

Several 2025 pieces argue portfolios matter less than distributed proof (posts, GitHub,
LinkedIn) — screening often happens before the site is opened. Not a reason to change the
site; a reason not to over-invest in length, and to make every page previewable (per-route
OG images, already decided in the plan).
