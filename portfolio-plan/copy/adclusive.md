## Why I took on a second role right as I started at McKinsey

In August 2021 I joined Adclusive — a Swedish startup building a platform to connect
advertisers with publishers and influencers — as their designer and a shareholder, part-time,
evenings and weekends. A month later I started at McKinsey.

The honest reason: I wanted to help build a real product, not just talk about wanting to.
McKinsey taught me how to communicate; Adclusive was where I could design something people
would actually log into. I kept both going for three years, until Adclusive wound down in
October 2024.

## What the platform had to do

Two very different audiences in one product. Advertisers create campaigns, upload creatives,
set collaboration rules, and track performance. Publishers and influencers browse
advertisers, manage tracking links, watch their revenue, and follow their money through
approval, invoicing, and payout. The business takes a commission on deals, so both sides
have to trust the numbers.

That's five kinds of complexity — campaign setup, tracking, creatives, performance, and
finance — that all wanted to sprawl into their own tools. The design job was keeping them in
one system a normal person could navigate.

## How the design held it together

I blocked out every screen in low-fidelity wireframes before any visual work, so navigation
and data hierarchy were settled arguments by the time things got pixels. The structure that
survived: separate role-based portals for the advertiser side and the publisher side,
sharing one consistent navigation, one layout logic, and one data hierarchy. The finance
screens got the most care — status buckets like *awaiting approval*, *approved*, and *paid*
sound boring until it's your payout in one of them.

We were 8 people in total. Day to day I worked closest with the two developers — one
frontend, one backend — turning flows into screens they could build without a formal handoff
process, because there wasn't one. When the API shaped what a screen could show, I learned
to design from the data outward instead of the mockup inward.

## Where it landed

The platform launched and got real: a month after launch it had 20+ active advertisers and
50+ influencers running campaigns through it. (Those are the launch-window numbers, from
2022 — I won't dress them up as current.) The platform paused in October 2024, a
funding-and-priorities story, not a product one.

## Why it's on this site

The screens below are from 2021–2023 and they look their age — label them by year and let
them. What this project shows isn't current visual craft; the two client projects handle
that. It shows that I could stay with one product through three years of real constraints,
a real team, and real users — years before AI tools made building things fast. The interest
was never the tools.
