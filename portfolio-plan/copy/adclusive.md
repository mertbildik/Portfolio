## A second role, before the company existed

In August 2021 I joined Adclusive as its designer and a shareholder, before the company
was even formally incorporated. A Swedish startup, a platform connecting advertisers with
publishers and influencers, and a team of eight. Part-time, evenings and weekends. A month
later I started at McKinsey.

The reason was simple: I wanted to build a real product. McKinsey taught me how to
communicate; Adclusive was where I could design something people would log into. I kept
both going for three years, until Adclusive wound down in October 2024.

## What the platform had to do

Advertisers create campaigns, upload creatives, set collaboration rules, and track
performance. Publishers and influencers browse campaigns, manage tracking links, and
follow their money from approval through invoicing to payout. The business takes a
commission on every deal.

That last part is the design problem. In a commission platform, both sides watch the same
numbers: the advertiser auditing what they owe, the publisher auditing what they're owed.
Tracking disputes are how platforms in this category lose their users. The numbers screens
aren't a feature of the product. They are the product.

## The money, made legible

So the finance screens got the most care. A publisher's earnings move through visible
states: awaiting approval, approved by advertiser, invoiced, ready for payment. Estimated
revenue and account balance sit apart, because "what I've earned" and "what I can
withdraw" are different questions with different answers. Boring words, until it's your
payout sitting in one of them.

> *Show: the shipped publisher dashboard, small.*
> Caption: `The shipped dashboard, 2022. Every kronor on it has a state a person can follow.`

The two sides each got their own portal, sharing one navigation, one layout logic, one
data hierarchy. That's the standard structure for this category, and it's standard for a
reason: in a product about trust, predictability is a feature. I followed the convention
deliberately.

## Structure settled before pixels

I blocked out every screen in low-fidelity wireframes before any visual work, so
navigation and data hierarchy were settled arguments by the time things got color. The
proof is visual:

> *Show: wireframe 2 and the shipped dashboard, side by side. The centerpiece of the page.*
> Caption: `Left: the wireframe, 2021. Right: the shipped screen, 2022. Same structure, box for box.`

Day to day I worked closest with the two developers, one frontend and one backend, turning
flows into screens they could build without a formal handoff process, because there wasn't
one. When the API shaped what a screen could show, I learned to design from the data
outward instead of the mockup inward.

## Where it landed

The platform launched in 2022 and got real: a month in, 20+ active advertisers and 50+
influencers were running campaigns through it. Those are launch-window numbers, and I
won't dress them up as current.

Adclusive paused in October 2024. For context, small Nordic ad networks were selling or
shutting down all that season, profitable ones included; the same month, a ten-person
Swedish network in the same business sold itself to the market leader. The product worked.
The market got hard.

## Why it's on this site

The screens below are from 2021 to 2023 and they look their age. They're labeled by year
and shown at the size of what they are: records. Current visual craft is what the two
client projects are for. This project shows something they can't: that I stayed with one
product through three years of real constraints, a real team, and real users, years before
AI tools made building things fast.
