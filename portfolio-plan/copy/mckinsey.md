## The NDA, up front

Client work at McKinsey is confidential, and I take that seriously. You won't find decks,
screenshots, or client names here. What I can share is how I worked, the decisions behind
the pages, and outcomes at a high level. On a call, I'm happy to go deeper into process:
how I handled charts, story flow, and review cycles.

## Three years, in numbers

- **10K+ assets**: slides, charts, templates, and systems, across three years of
  consultant teams.
- **50+ pitch decks**: partner- and C-suite-ready, often on brutal timelines.
- **3 major initiatives**: bigger work beyond the day-to-day lane.
- **2 native app tests**: UI/UX feedback rounds where the teams shipped the changes.

## What the job actually was

The core skill is compression. A consultant hands you a messy model and a deadline, and
the page that comes back has to make one point, fast, to someone with thirty seconds and
the authority to say no. Most of my time went to exactly that: data visualization with
clean labels and honest scales, slide flow that survives being skimmed, and titles that
state the decision instead of describing the chart.

The rest was the work around the work: one-on-one sessions with consultants, live group
reviews where decisions got made in the room, and handoff habits that kept decks clean
after I rolled off. Toward the end I also joined user tests for internal native apps,
writing feedback concrete enough that the teams shipped it.

## What it left me with

A permanent intolerance for pages that make the reader work. Everything I've built since
runs on the same instinct: figure out what the reader needs to verify, and put it where
they can't miss it. On OFK that became a site that leads with signed reference letters. On
Sinerjik, a site that demos the product instead of describing it. Same skill, new medium.
